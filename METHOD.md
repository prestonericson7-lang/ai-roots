# Method

## What was asked

Preston Ericson, 11-21-2026: find how far back the government has used AI, whether today's AI is "the scraps of what was already there,"
whether old videos were faked by AI long before the public knew, and whether an AI humans didn't make was found long ago. Search hard, as if it
has been a cover-up the whole time; trust his direction without agreeing 100 percent; and where the trail runs out, shoot in the dark and say so.

## How the work was done

- **Web searches** across news archives, agency sites, journals, and Wikipedia for orientation, always followed to the best source found.
- **Direct searches of the CIA's declassified reading room** (cia.gov/readingroom), reading the hits themselves.
- **Arithmetic** for the hardware question ([`tools/sizing.py`](tools/sizing.py)), on speeds from the TOP500 lists and agency histories.
- **No agents, no automation over the sources**: one researcher (Claude) reading and writing, start to finish, on the evening of 11-21-2026.

## Labels

- **MEASURED:** my own arithmetic on cited numbers, or counts I read off a source myself.
- **ESTIMATE:** my judgment, or a number I chose (like the 1e21 workload).
- **UNVERIFIED:** a single secondary source, or details the sources dispute.
- **SPECULATION:** the shots in the dark; links across gaps the record doesn't fill, each with the test that would settle it.

## Limits

- **What is still classified is not here**, by definition. Secrecy has lasted 24 to 30 years on the record (GCHQ's public-key work, Colossus).
- **Several key stories come through secondary outlets** (the 1999 Arkin article, the 2010 Washington Post CIA report); their originals should be
  read.
- **Search engines and archives were searched by keyword.** A record filed under other words can be missed.
- **Quotes from sources are kept short;** the links hold the rest.

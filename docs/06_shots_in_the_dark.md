# Shots in the dark

Preston asked for this chapter: "This isn't gonna have a clean trail at every turn. You'll have to shoot in the dark here and there to make links
as you go. Vetted or not, that's half the issue: the gaps are large and undocumented enough to speculate nonexistence, but I argue it's just a good
cover-up."

Everything below is **SPECULATION**: links across gaps the record does not fill. Each shot says what it connects, what supports it, what cuts
against it, and the test that would settle it. A shot is not a finding; it is a place to dig next.

---

## 1. The late-1990s window for secret synthetic video

**The link.** Pentagon planners wanted digital morphing of voice, video and photos for psychological operations from 1990 on, and Los Alamos made
a convincing fake recording of a general from minutes of his real speech by 1999 ([Arkin, Washington Post, February 1, 1999, via Privacy SOS](https://privacysos.org/blog/technology-to-spoof-human-voices-has-been-around-for-over-fifteen-years/)).
In the open, Video Rewrite (1997) already made existing footage mouth new words automatically, with JFK footage among its examples
([the paper](https://www.mit.edu/~9.520/spring09/Papers/VideoRewrite.pdf)). And the NSA has held compute leads of 50 to 200 times the best
commercial machine (Harvest, 1962). With a lead that size, a small modern generative model trains in 12 to 47 days on year-2000 hardware
(see [02_sizing.md](02_sizing.md)). Put together: a classified program could have made convincing talking-head fakes of real people by about
1998 to 2003.

**Supports it.** The motive (psyops since 1990), the method in the open (1997), the hardware with a secret lead (about 2000), the money
($52.6 billion a year for intelligence in 2013), and a working voice fake at a national lab (1999).

**Cuts against it.** The one fake video the CIA is reported to have made, in 2003, used CIA employees as actors; a team with good synthetic video
would not have needed them. The 2013 Snowden archive shows machine learning (SKYNET) but, as far as reported, no synthetic people.

**The test.** FOIA requests for "digital morphing" and "voice morphing" records at DoD and Los Alamos, 1990 to 2005; the original text of Arkin's
1999 article; the searchable Snowden archives for "morph" and "synthetic".

## 2. The AI winter was only public

**The link.** Public AI money crashed more than once (machine translation after 1966, Britain after the 1973 Lighthill report, neural networks
after 1969). Classified interest did not: the CIA paid for perceptrons on spy photos in 1963 to 1964 (Project PIGS), the NSA's journal ran
pattern-recognition papers in 1961 and 1977, and the CIA worked on machine translation from the 1950s into the 1980s
([03_archives.md](03_archives.md)). The speculation: neural-network work never stopped inside the agencies, so working neural pattern
recognition on imagery and signals may have existed in the 1970s and 80s, years before the open revival in 1986.

**Supports it.** The PIGS reports end with a Mark II perceptron design, and a later contract extends "perceptron applicability to automatic photo
interpretation"; 33 NGA records in the CIA archive mention perceptrons.

**Cuts against it.** The 1983 to 1986 intelligence-community AI papers read as catching up with commercial expert systems, not as owners of
something better.

**The test.** Follow the NGA perceptron trail in the CIA reading room, contract by contract, 1964 to 1985; FOIA NPIC and NGA records on
neural networks and "adaptive" pattern recognition.

## 3. One Air Force office for foreign technology, UFOs and machine translation

**The link.** The Air Technical Intelligence Center at Wright-Patterson ran Project Blue Book, the Air Force's UFO investigation, and one CIA record
has it "vitally interested" in machine translation; its successor, the Foreign Technology Division, reverse-engineered foreign hardware as its
everyday job. If non-human material had ever been held, this is where the bureaucracy, the people and the era's best computing and translation
tools sat together.

**Supports it.** The office's two jobs are both on the record; FTD's reverse-engineering role is its stated mission.

**Cuts against it.** AARO's 2024 review found no off-world technology and the companies named denied ever holding any; Corso's specific claims
don't hold up against his records.

**The test.** FOIA ATIC and FTD records on "materials of unknown origin"; the National Archives' UAP Records Collection as it is released under
the FY2024 NDAA.

## 4. Classified twins of public AI

**The link.** Siri came from DARPA's CALO (2003); Total Information Awareness was killed in public in 2003 and reportedly kept alive at the NSA as
"Basketball" and "Topsail"; the NRO's Sentient has run since about 2010. The speculation: many public AI capabilities have a classified twin a few
years ahead in narrow uses: imagery, speech, text.

**Supports it.** Every one of those cases is documented; IARPA's program goals regularly assume capabilities just ahead of the open papers.

**Cuts against it.** The open frontier since 2017 came from commercial labs whose compute purchases dwarf any known government AI budget, and the
government now buys frontier models from industry (Claude Gov, 2025).

**The test.** Line up each DARPA and IARPA program's stated goal and start date against the first public paper for that capability, and look for
programs whose goals assume a capability before the open world had it.

## 5. Lost and found again

**The link.** The Antikythera mechanism's epicyclic gearing (about 100 BC) vanished for about 1,500 years; Göbekli Tepe shows monuments 11,000 or
more years old; the Silurian paper says an older industrial civilization might leave little more than an isotope spike. The speculation: knowledge,
computation included, may have existed and been lost more than once, and myths of made minds (Talos, the golem, the old automata) are what's left
of it.

**Supports it.** Loss is documented (Antikythera); the rock record is thin enough that scientists published the question.

**Cuts against it.** No artifact of electronic computing is older than the 1940s, and no industrial residue beyond known natural events has been
found.

**The test.** The Silurian paper's own proposals: anomalous isotope ratios and unusual elements in sediment layers around past warm spikes such as
the PETM.

## 6. A non-human mind behind the curtain

**The link.** Congress now orders records on "technologies of unknown origin" and "non-human intelligence"; a sworn witness (David Grusch, 2023)
told of a retrieval program; AATIP's contractor stored alloys said to come from UAPs. Preston's thread: if a non-human technology was held, its
machine intelligence would be its most guarded part, and its "scraps," as ideas more than code, might have seeded human computing.

**Supports it.** The legal category exists; the testimony was under oath; secrecy of 24 to 30 years is on the record for lesser things.

**Cuts against it.** Every computing step traced so far has a human trail; the transistor's idea predates Roswell by 22 years (Lilienfeld, 1925);
AARO found nothing; Grusch's evidence has not been shown in public.

**The test.** What the UAP Records Collection releases; AARO volume 2; any declassified inventory of "materials of unknown origin".

## 7. The 2003 actors, read both ways

**The link.** The CIA's 2003 bin Laden fake used CIA employees as actors. Read one way, it means the CIA had no good synthetic video then (it cuts
against shot 1). Read the other way, it means the CIA's Iraq Operations Group was not cleared for a capability held elsewhere, at the NSA or in
DoD.

**Supports the second reading.** Compartments inside the government are real: TIA's parts reportedly moved to the NSA under new names after
Congress killed it.

**The test.** Nothing public settles it; it marks the kind of gap where a capability can be hidden from the government itself.

## 8. The bad clip on purpose

**The link.** Preston's idea: the clumsy public AI videos (the 2023 spaghetti clip) taught the public to underestimate what existed.

**Supports it.** Public perception of AI video was set by that clip for a year; the 2018 BuzzFeed Obama fake was framed as a warning.

**Cuts against it.** The spaghetti clip came from a random Reddit user with an open-source model from Alibaba's research lab (ModelScope), and far
better public models appeared within months; no sign of coordination turned up.

**The test.** Trace who funded and released each widely seen early generator, and whether any government had 2023-level video earlier (shot 1's
tests).

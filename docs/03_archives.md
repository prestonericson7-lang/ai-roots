# Into the archives

Searched directly on 11-21-2026: the CIA's declassified reading room ([cia.gov/readingroom](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/)), with the NSA's
declassified technical journal and NSA history documents found through web search. Counts are what the reading room reported that evening.

## CIA reading room

| Search | Documents | What they show |
|---|---|---|
| ["artificial intelligence"](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/search/site/%22artificial%20intelligence%22) | 644 | An organized intelligence-community AI effort from 1983: an AI Steering Group formed in February 1983 by the Intelligence R&D Council (chaired, per the documents, by Richard D. DeLauer; draft charter April 22, 1983); the CIA's AI Applications Working Group, chartered to bring AI into the Agency (second meeting August 23, 1983); yearly AI symposia from December 1983 at CIA headquarters to March 1985 at the new DIA building, where 17 companies exhibited AI products; an AI staff in the CIA's Office of Information Technology by December 1986. The agencies were buying the AI of their day, expert systems from companies like Teknowledge. 45 of the hits sit in the STARGATE collection. |
| ["perceptron"](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/search/site/perceptron) | 101 | Project PIGS, June 1963 to July 1964: the CIA's photo interpreters paid Cornell Aeronautical Laboratory (Rosenblatt's lab) to apply perceptrons to reconnaissance photos; the Mark I recognized silhouetted military targets; a Mark II was designed; coordinated with the Navy's photo center; declassified after NGA review in 2005 (final report CIA-RDP80B01139A000500240008-5). The CIA also abstracted Soviet perceptron papers, Ivakhnenko's among them. 33 of the hits are NGA records. |
| ["machine translation"](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/search/site/%22machine%20translation%22) | 649 | Machine translation of Russian and Chinese from the 1950s (monthly reports from May 1960 and December 1963); a paper by CIA deputy assistant director Paul W. Howerton on what an operational system would need; a list of 342 Soviet linguists; a 1976 seminar; money for Japanese machine translation in 1985. One record has the Air Technical Intelligence Center, the Air Force office that ran Project Blue Book, "vitally interested" in machine translation. |
| ["generative artificial intelligence"](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/search/site/%22generative%20artificial%20intelligence%22) | 2 | "Evaluating the Application of Generative Artificial Intelligence to OSINT," released September 22, 2026, and an unrelated older letter whose keyword list includes AI. |
| ["voice synthesis" OR deepfake OR "synthetic video"](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/search/site/%22voice%20synthesis%22%20OR%20deepfake%20OR%20%22synthetic%20video%22) | 5 | Translations of public Japanese technology reports (a 1981 voice-synthesis board among them) and a budget plan; nothing about the government making synthetic people. |

## What the archives add (ESTIMATE)

In the released record, the agencies' machine-mind work runs in step with the open science of its day: perceptrons on spy photos in 1963,
machine translation from the 1950s, expert systems in the 1980s, generative AI evaluations now. Where secrecy hid a lead, the lead is years, not
decades (crypto: 3 to 16 years ahead; compute: Harvest, 50 to 200 times). What a released archive cannot show is what is still classified;
Colossus (30 years) and GCHQ's public-key work (24 years) measure how long that can last.
